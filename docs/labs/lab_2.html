Lab 2: Descriptive Statistics - Central Tendency & Data Visualization

Sleep, Test Anxiety, and Memory During Exam Week

Published

September 9, 2026

Overview & Scenario

A psychology research team is studying student well-being during exam week. They recruited 24 undergraduate students and recorded each participant’s self-reported sleep from the prior night, test-anxiety score, performance on a short memory-recall task, and primary coping strategy.

The team’s research question is:

During exam week, what are the typical levels of sleep, test anxiety, and memory recall among undergraduate students, and which coping strategy is most common?

This is a descriptive question. We will use the mean, median, and mode to describe the sample appropriately. We will also use visualizations to help us understand the distributions of the variables. Finally, we will write a concise APA-style paragraph to summarize the results.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lab, you should be able to:

  1. Import a excel format dataset into SPSS and set up variable names, value labels, and measurement levels.
    Select the mean, median, or mode that best represents a variable.
  2. Use SPSS to create frequency tables, descriptive statistics, and histograms.
  3. Explain how an extreme score influences the mean and median.
  4. Write a concise APA-style descriptive-results paragraph.

  • Click Open. In the pop-up window, ensure that Read variable names from the first row of data is checked. Click OK. An output pop up window may appear but you can ignore it and proceed to the data window. The dataset should now appear in SPSS.

  • In Variable View, enter the value labels and measurement levels below.

Part B — Analyzing Continuous Variables

Central Tendency

We want to find the “typical hours of sleep” for the sampled students by finding the Mean, Mode, and Median using SPSS. Follow the following steps:

  • Analyze > Descriptive Statistics > Frequencies.
  • Move sleep_hours to the Variable(s) box.
  • Click Statistics, and check Mode, Meam, and Median
  • Click Continue, then OK. Keep the frequency table in your output.
Checkpoint
  1. Report the three measures of center.
  2. Which of the three measures is the most reliable and why?

Visualizing Continuous Variables (Sleep Hours)

To visualize the distribution of a continuous variable such as sleep_hours, a histogram is one of the most reliable tools. Follow the following steps to create a histogram in SPSS:

  • Analyze > Descriptive Statistics > Frequencies.
  • Move sleep_hours to the Variable(s) box.
  • Click Charts, select Histograms, and check Display normal curve.
  • Click Continue, then OK. Keep the histogram in your output.
Checkpoint

How would you describe the distribution of sleep hours? Is the histogram roughly symmetric, positively skewed, or negatively skewed?

Another tool that can be used to visualize the distribution of a continuous variable is a boxplot. Follow the following steps to create a boxplot in SPSS:

  • Graphs > Chart Builder.
  • Select Boxplot from the gallery, then drag the simple boxplot icon to the chart preview area.
  • Drag sleep_hours to the y-axis and click OK. Keep the box plot in your output.
Checkpoint

How would you describe the distribution of sleep hours based on the box plot?

Extreme Scores

Presence of extreme scores (outliers) can influence central tendency. Change the sleet hours for the last participant (id = 24) to 20 hours. Re-run the analysis for sleep_hours and record the new mean, median, and mode. - How does this change affect these values? In this case, which value is more reliable? - How do the distributions change based on the histogram and box plot?

Simple Transformations on Continuous Variables

Now, imagine the research team revises the anxiety scores by adding 5 points to every participant’s score. We can do this in SPSS as follows:

  • Transform > Compute Variable.
  • Under Target Variable, write anxiety_plus_5.
  • Under Numeric Expression write Test_Anxiety_Score + 5.
  • Click OK.

Look at the data file and you should see a new variable called anxiety_plus_5.

Run the Mean and median for for Test_Anxiety_Score and anxiety_plus_5.

Checkpoint

How did the mean and median anxiety change after adding 5 to each individual score? State the rule for what happens to the mean and median when the same constant is added to every score.

Part C — Analyzing Discrete Variables

In most cases, summary statistics such as the mean and median are not useful for discrete variables measured at the nominal or ordinal level. This is because it doesnt make mathematical sense to add two categories (e.g Male +Female). For these variables, the mode can be used to tell us the category with the highest representation (frequency). A frequency distribution table can be made to identify this.

Frequency Distribution Table for Discrete Variables

A frequency Distribution table is a straight forward tool that can be used to summarize and organize a discrete nominal variable such as Primary_Coping_Startegy. To make the table in SPSS, follow the following steps:

[Be sure to clear the previous analyses before running this one. For example, disable Histograms]

  • Analyze > Descriptive Statistics > Frequencies.
  • Move Primary_Coping_Strategy to the Variable(s) box.
  • Check the Display frequency tables box.
  • Click OK.

Visualizing Discrete Variables

To visualize a discrete variable such as Primary_Coping_Strategy, a bar chart is one of the most reliable tools. Follow the following steps to create a bar chart in SPSS:

  • Analyze > Descriptive Statistics > Frequencies.
  • Move Primary_Coping_Strategy to the Variable(s) box.
  • Click Charts, select Bar charts,
  • Uncheck the Display frequency tables box.
  • Click OK.

Keep the bar chart in your output.

Tip

Based on the frequency table and bar chart, which coping strategy was the most common among the sample? Report the mode in words, along with its frequency and percentage.

Part D — Analyzing Multiple Variables

Although analyzing variables separately is useful, the complexity of life often require us to look at multiple variables together. For example, we may want to know if students who sleep more hours have lower test anxiety scores. To do this, we can use a scatterplot to visualize the relationship between two continuous variables.

Here is how to create a scatterplot for sleep hours vs test anxiety scores in SPSS:

  • Graphs > Chart Builder.
  • Select Scatter/Dot from the gallery, then drag the simple scatter icon to the chart preview area.
  • Drag Sleep_Hours_Prior_Night to the x-axis and Test_Anxiety_Score to the y-axis. Click OK. Keep the scatterplot in your output.

We may also want to compare the mean test anxiety scores by coping strategy. To do this, we can use a bar chart to visualize the mean test anxiety scores for each coping strategy.

  • Graphs > Chart Builder.
  • Select Bar from the gallery, then drag the simple bar icon to the chart preview
  • Drag Primary_Coping_Strategy to the x-axis and Test_Anxiety_Score to the y-axis. Click OK. By default, SPSS will calculate the mean for each group. Keep the bar chart in your output.
Checkpoint
  • Based on the scatterplot, is there a relationship between sleep hours and test anxiety scores?
  • Based on the bar chart, which coping strategy is associated with the lowest mean test anxiety score?
  • Save your SPSS output file as Lab_2_Output_YourLastName.spv.

APA-Style Reporting Tips

APA-style results writing should be accurate, concise, and tied to the research question. Use the statistics you produced in SPSS—not estimates from the graph.

  • Report the sample size as N when referring to the whole sample.
  • Report the mean as M and the standard deviation as SD.
  • Report the median as Mdn when it is the better summary for a skewed distribution.
  • Report a categorical mode in words, followed by its frequency (n) and percentage when useful.
  • Round consistently, typically to two decimal places unless your instructor directs otherwise.
  • Describe the distribution when that explains why you selected the median instead of the mean.
